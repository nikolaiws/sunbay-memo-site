Questions for the CTO call (Swap test and FETCH: Failures, Evidence, Team, Cost, Hard-to-copy)

  • Swap. Swap the voice model (ElevenLabs) and the reports model: does any customer notice? Good answer: no, the paths and ERP sync are the product.
  • Failures. Top failure classes in payment matching and reminder sending, by frequency. Good: 'x% bank-statement mismatches, y% wrong contact, z% ERP sync lag'.
  • Evals. Is there a held-out set of real reminder threads and matches, refreshed from production? Good: yes, scored per release.
  • Touchless. Share of reminders, promises-to-pay and case closures with no human touch, by customer cohort, and the trend.
  • Cost. Cost per automated action today (email, SMS, voice minute) against the per-invoice price; gross margin after inference.
  • Handoff. When the voice agent or matcher fails, who gets the case, how fast, and what the customer sees.
  • Tenx. At 10x invoices, what breaks first: ERP connector rate limits, SMS cost, the human review queue?
  • Who Built It. Did Nikodem write the ERP connectors and the matching core, or an agency?
Method

How this was made

Built by Nico with Claude Code (Claude Fable 5.1) for research, drafting and code. Every sourced number links to its source with the access date; derived figures carry the E tag and show their inputs. The judgment and the call are mine. Mirror: https://nikolaiws.github.io/sunbay-memo-site/.

Where I overruled the draft. It led with Carta's US seed median as the reason the price was cheap; that is the wrong comparator for a Polish pre-seed, so the lead is now the implied ARR multiple and the ownership the cheque buys. It called the June coverage 'paying customers'; tech.eu says business customers, so every bullet was reworded and the ARR is derived, not reported. It cited Serrala for the $200M regional exit, whose price is undisclosed; that number is my estimate between YayPay and Sidetrade. It set the small-M&A case at $28M; YayPay was 'more than €17M' for 87%, so $22M. It put seed-to-Series-A conversion beside the $200M case as if they were the same event; that bullet is now the flip-point analysis. Two risks carried 'mitigation: none'; a risk an investor cannot mitigate gets priced into the cheque or written into the terms, so they now do. The call moved from Pass to Invest on two numbers: the ownership $1.2M buys at this price, and the category's two take-privates above $1.6B. Its last draft said Invest at $6M on a 3x bar; a $70M fund needs about 4.3x gross from each line, and on that bar the Invest-now against track-and-re-enter arithmetic moved the call to Pass at these terms with a priced re-entry at the seed. The 4.3x bar itself came from the review pass; I re-did the fund arithmetic (3x net after 2% fees and 20% carry on $56M to $59.5M invested) and adopted it because it is what each line has to return, not what the fund returns.

Method, tools and cost
  1. Sources in: the deck (poppler, 12 slides) and 86 web pages and PDFs fetched with httpx and kept as text with their access dates, including sunbay.io, the June 2026 round coverage, GUS and Eurostat firm counts, BIG InfoMonitor and Intrum payment data, thirteen international competitors and six Polish incumbents, exit comparables and base rates.
  2. Numbers out: data/benchmarks.json, comparables.json and competitors.json, each row with a source id and an evidence tier set by the analyst.
  3. Research pass: seven research notes on memo craft, interactive models, page design, hosting and AI screeners, folded into one decisions report before any code.
  4. Decision frame: ownership path, fund-returner exit, scenario tree anchored on base rates and comparables, what has to be true, kill criteria, steelman cases, pre-mortem; written before the memo bullets.
  5. Review: Claude agents in five roles read the page and the sources only (a sceptical s16vc partner, a numbers checker, a design reviewer, a red team on the investment logic, a brief-compliance editor); their findings and what changed are in the review log below.
  6. Build: Python owns the maths and writes the fixtures the browser JavaScript is tested against; the page renders its base case without JavaScript; charts are hand-written SVG; a citation checker searches every number in a third-party or company bullet in its cited source text.
  7. Team Brain (s16vc's own tool) hosts this page as a site and runs the compiled Inbound screener: its v1 scored Sunbay 1/1/1 from the site alone and failed closed; the rebuilt judge (fixed questions, verified quotes, three votes) scores 3/5/3, MEET at 93% agreement, and is what runs there now. On the fund's 16 eval companies it moved from 12/16 to 13/16 with all eight synthetic archetypes right; the full eval sits in the screener repository.
  8. Kev-0.8B, tried as an evidence-tier gate: it agreed with the analyst on 32 of 60 numbers and changed no tier, so it stayed out of the critical path, as did GLiNER.
